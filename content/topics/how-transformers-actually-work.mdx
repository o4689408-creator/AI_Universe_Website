In 2017, a small team of researchers set out to solve a narrow problem in machine translation. What they built instead became the architecture underneath nearly every major AI system released since — from chatbots to image generators to code assistants.

## Why the transformer mattered

Before transformers, sequence models read text one word at a time, carrying context forward step by step. That worked, but it was slow to train and struggled to remember anything far back in a sentence. The transformer's core idea — attention — let a model look at every word in a sequence simultaneously and decide which ones mattered most to each other.

<PullQuote>
Attention doesn't process language in order — it processes it in relationship.
</PullQuote>

That single shift made models both faster to train and dramatically better at capturing long-range meaning, which is why the architecture scaled so well as it grew larger.

## The core mechanism: self-attention

Self-attention gives every word in a sentence a way to "look at" every other word and weigh how relevant each one is. When a model reads "the trophy didn't fit in the suitcase because it was too big," self-attention is what lets it correctly connect "it" back to "trophy" rather than "suitcase."

<Callout title="Go deeper: queries, keys, and values">

Each word is projected into three vectors: a query (what am I looking for?), a key (what do I contain?), and a value (what information do I offer?). A word's query is compared against every other word's key to produce attention scores, which are then used to blend the values into a new representation. Stack this across multiple "heads" running in parallel — multi-head attention — and the model can track several different kinds of relationships at once (grammar, meaning, coreference) simultaneously.

</Callout>

## Stacking layers, building understanding

A single attention layer captures simple relationships. Real models stack dozens of these layers, each one refining the representation a little further — early layers tend to capture syntax and local structure, while deeper layers build toward more abstract meaning.

## Why this architecture won

Transformers scaled better than anything that came before them, largely because their core computation — matrix multiplication across an entire sequence at once — maps extremely well onto GPU hardware. That combination of architectural elegance and hardware fit is arguably the single biggest reason today's AI progress moved as fast as it did.

<DidYouKnow>

The original transformer paper, "Attention Is All You Need," was published in 2017 by a team of eight researchers at Google. Several of its authors went on to found or join other major AI labs — the architecture's influence on the field extends well beyond the paper itself.

</DidYouKnow>

## Key takeaways

<KeyTakeaways>

- Self-attention lets a model weigh the relevance of every word to every other word, all at once.
- Multi-head attention tracks several types of relationships in parallel.
- Stacked layers build from concrete syntax toward abstract meaning.
- The architecture's fit with GPU hardware is a major reason it scaled so well.

</KeyTakeaways>
